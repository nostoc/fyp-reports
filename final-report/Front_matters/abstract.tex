\chapter*{Abstract}


Local governments in developing countries often struggle to address public issues efficiently, mainly due to centralized data control, lack of transparency and insufficient citizen participation. While crowdsourced reporting systems and e-governance platforms are designed to encourage citizen engagement, their effectiveness is limited due to concerns related to data integrity, user anonymity and content reliability. This project presents a community-based civic reporting system that addresses these challenges using a private permissioned \ac{PoA} blockchain integrated with off-chain storage, an \ac{AI} based content moderation service, smart contracts and a Web3 \ac{DApp}. User identities are decoupled from the report content using a simulated identity and \ac{RBAC} mechanism to preserve citizen privacy. This system enables citizens to submit public issue
reports privately and participate in opinion polling. The underlying blockchain ensures data immutability and transparency. To reduce on-chain storage overhead, reporting data are hosted off-chain on \ac{IPFS} with their \ac{CID} hashes anchored on-chain. Before publication, text and image submissions are screened with \ac{NSFW} and adult-content classifiers so that clearly unsuitable material is less likely to appear on the platform.
Performance tests on the implemented prototype, show acceptable latency and stable on-chain anchoring, suggesting the design is workable for community-level civic reporting in local government settings.
