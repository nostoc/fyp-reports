\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This chapter concludes the research, architectural design, and engineering implementation of the blockchain-based, community-assisted, privacy-preserving reporting framework for local governance. It synthesizes the primary research contributions and system innovations, evaluates the empirical insights and technical trade-offs validated during prototype development, discusses the limitations of the current implementation, and outlines strategic directions for future academic research, protocol extensions, and real-world municipal deployment.

\section{Summary of Research and Engineering Contributions}
\label{sec:summary-contributions}

Conventional centralized civic reporting platforms—such as municipal hotlines, monolithic e-governance web portals, and commercial systems like \textit{FixMyStreet} or \textit{SeeClickFix}—suffer from systemic limitations. These include opaque resolution workflows, centralized data silos, vulnerability to arbitrary administrative dismissal, lack of inter-agency accountability, and citizen reluctance to report sensitive public infrastructure or governance failures due to fear of reprisal. This thesis formulated, designed, and implemented a decentralized, privacy-first civic engagement ecosystem that addresses these challenges through a six-layer hybrid on-chain/off-chain architecture.

The primary research and engineering contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decentralized Lifecycle Accounting via Deterministic Finite State Machine (FSM):}
    The project engineered an eight-state deterministic \ac{FSM} within the \texttt{Reporting.sol} smart contract ($S_0 = \texttt{PendingValidation}$ through $S_7 = \texttt{Reopened}$). By enforcing strict Checks-Effects-Interactions (CEI) design patterns and tracking single-use cryptographic ticket nullifiers across persistent mappings (\texttt{usedSubmissionNullifiers}, \texttt{usedValidationVoteNullifiers}, \texttt{usedVerificationVoteNullifiers}, and \texttt{usedRejectionReviewVoteNullifiers}), the ledger guarantees an immutable, tamper-evident audit trail of every civic report while mathematically preventing Sybil attacks and double-voting.

    \item \textbf{Two-Tier Multi-Signature Governance and On-Chain Profile Directory:}
    To prevent unilateral administrative control and establish civic transparency, access control was decentralized through \texttt{AuthorityMultiSig.sol}, to which ownership of \texttt{Reporting.sol} is transferred upon deployment. This contract establishes a two-tier governance hierarchy: Super Admins (high-ranking municipal officials voting with a dynamic majority quorum $\lfloor \texttt{superAdminCount}/2 \rfloor + 1$) and Authority Workers (municipal staff executing physical infrastructure repairs). Every official is assigned an immutable on-chain identity profile (\texttt{name}, \texttt{position}, \texttt{department}), enabling citizens to verify the exact institutional authority responsible for resolving a reported issue.

    \item \textbf{Zero-Knowledge Citizen Authentication and Identity Decoupling:}
    To eliminate citizen fear of retaliation without compromising platform integrity, the system integrated a Zero-Knowledge Proof (\ac{ZKP}) GovID Simulator. Citizens authenticate against an official municipal registry to receive a batch of randomized, single-use ticket identifiers (\texttt{zkpTicketId}) signed by the government authority's ECDSA private key. By deriving deterministic citizen pseudonyms ($\text{keccak256}(\text{citizenPubKey} \parallel \text{DOMAIN\_SALT})$) on the client side, the platform achieves complete decoupling between real-world civic identities and on-chain reporting activities.

    \item \textbf{Gasless Relayer Middleware and Automated Orchestration:}
    To remove cryptocurrency onboarding barriers (such as wallet installation, gas token acquisition, and RPC configuration), the architecture implemented a NestJS Backend Relayer. The relayer features a Dual-Layer Cryptographic Verification Engine that validates government ticket ECDSA signatures and citizen payload signatures before dispatching gas-sponsored transactions to the blockchain. Furthermore, the relayer incorporates two automated backend orchestration services:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Authority Funding Service (\texttt{AuthorityFundingService}):} An event-driven listener that monitors \texttt{ProposalExecuted} events on \texttt{AuthorityMultiSig.sol} and automatically transfers native ETH top-ups to newly approved Authority Worker accounts, seamlessly bridging on-chain access control with off-chain operational readiness.
        \item \textit{Background Cron Orchestration (\texttt{CronService}):} Scheduled automated tasks that monitor authority wallet balances and execute daily midnight batch finalizations (\texttt{batchFinalizeVotingWindows}) for reports in expired voting phases ($S_0$, $S_4$, and $S_5$), preventing workflow stagnation.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Off-Chain P2P Media Storage and Multi-Oracle AI Content Moderation:}
    To prevent ledger bloat and mitigate spam, abusive language, or non-civic noise before on-chain anchoring, the architecture decoupled state management from data storage. High-resolution photographic evidence is anchored to peer-to-peer \ac{IPFS} storage (\texttt{ipfsCid}), while report text and images are evaluated by an interconnected AI Moderation Oracle Ensemble (\texttt{Safety Oracle} using fine-tuned \texttt{unitary/toxic-bert}, \texttt{Spam and Abuse Oracle} using \texttt{sms-spam-detection}, \texttt{Civic Relevance Oracle} using \texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, and a coordinating \texttt{Oracle Aggregator}).

    \item \textbf{Democratic Lifecycle Oversight and Anonymous Public Consultation:}
    The framework empowers citizens at critical decision junctions: peer validation voting ($S_0$) filters spam, post-repair verification voting ($S_5$) prevents authorities from closing unresolved reports, and rejection review voting ($S_4$) allows citizens to appeal improper issue dismissals. Additionally, the integrated \texttt{OpinionPolling.sol} contract enables municipal authorities to conduct Sybil-resistant, anonymous public opinion consultations on municipal proposals and budget allocations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Synthesis of Key Findings and Engineering Validation}
\label{sec:synthesis-findings}

The development and evaluation of the prototype yielded several critical insights into the design of decentralized civic governance systems:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reconciling Public Transparency with Citizen Privacy:}
    A foundational challenge in public blockchain applications is that immutable on-chain records inherently conflict with user privacy and data protection requirements. This research demonstrated that single-use cryptographic ticket nullifiers combined with government-issued ECDSA signatures successfully bridge this gap. Citizens can publicly prove their entitlement to report issues and vote on municipal matters without linking their transactions to a static wallet address or revealing personally identifiable information (\ac{PII}).

    \item \textbf{Middleware Abstraction as a Prerequisite for Civic Adoption:}
    Empirical evaluation confirmed that requiring citizens to interact directly with decentralized ledgers creates an insurmountable usability barrier for municipal applications. By delegating transaction signing, gas sponsorship, and RPC connection management to an off-chain backend relayer, the system achieves standard web-application latency and usability (via the Next.js presentation layer) while preserving the cryptographic verification and censorship-resistance guarantees of the underlying blockchain.

    \item \textbf{Automated Cron Finalizations Complement Lazy Evaluation:}
    In decentralized smart contracts, state transitions typically rely on "lazy evaluation"—executing state updates when a subsequent user transaction interacts with the contract. However, in civic reporting, reports with zero voting activity after a phase deadline could remain indefinitely pending. Testing confirmed that combining lazy evaluation with daily automated batch finalizations via \texttt{CronService.batchFinalizeVotingWindows} is essential for guaranteeing deterministic lifecycle progression without requiring continuous manual administrative intervention.

    \item \textbf{Efficacy of Multi-Oracle Pre-Submission Filtering:}
    Benchmarking the AI Moderation Oracle Ensemble demonstrated that off-chain machine learning verification is vital for protecting private permissioned ledgers from spam, flood attacks, and irrelevant content. By filtering toxicity and non-civic reports at the relayer gateway, the system prevents immutable ledger pollution and ensures that municipal engineering teams focus exclusively on actionable civic infrastructure repairs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations of the Current Prototype}
\label{sec:limitations}

While the prototype successfully demonstrates the feasibility of a privacy-preserving, blockchain-based local governance platform, several technical and architectural limitations remain:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Simulated Zero-Knowledge Identity Infrastructure:}
    In the current implementation, citizen authentication and ticket signing are executed via the ZKP GovID Simulator—a Node.js/Express service simulating a national identity registry. Although the cryptographic ticket verification protocol faithfully replicates zero-knowledge attestation, real-world deployment requires integration with a production zero-knowledge proving circuit where citizens generate zero-knowledge proofs locally on their devices without trusting a mock server.

    \item \textbf{Co-Located Oracle Aggregation Topology:}
    While the AI Moderation Ensemble logically separates Safety, Spam, and Civic Relevance verification into distinct worker modules, the current prototype deploys these services within a single administrative environment. As a result, the oracle layer currently acts as an administratively co-located verification gateway rather than a truly decentralized, multi-organizational oracle network.

    \item \textbf{Unimodal Textual Moderation Pipeline:}
    The AI content moderation pipeline currently relies on unimodal natural language processing (NLP) models to evaluate textual descriptions. While visual evidence is evaluated for adult or graphic content, the prototype lacks a multimodal reasoning model capable of cross-verifying whether an uploaded image semantically matches the citizen's selected issue category and textual description.

    \item \textbf{Permissioned Consortium Scale and Validator Latency:}
    The underlying blockchain operates as an Ethereum-compatible Clique Proof-of-Authority (\ac{PoA}) network across a three-validator consortium (Municipal Authority, Regional Oversight, and Civic NGO nodes). While this topology provides rapid block finality and zero energy cost compared to Proof-of-Work (\ac{PoW}), the network throughput and consensus stability under large-scale municipal transaction volumes (e.g., thousands of simultaneous citizen votes during a city-wide emergency) require further empirical stress testing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Directions for Future Research and System Extensions}
\label{sec:future-work}

To address the limitations of the current prototype and transition the framework toward production readiness, future research and development should focus on four key strategic directions:

\subsection{Production Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) and Self-Sovereign Identity (SSI) Protocol Integration}
\label{subsec:future-zkp-ssi}

Future iterations should replace the ZKP GovID Simulator with a production-grade **Self-Sovereign Identity (\ac{ssi})** framework conforming to W3C Decentralized Identifier (DID) and Verifiable Credential (VC) standards. Specifically, integrating open-source zero-knowledge identity protocols such as **Semaphore** or **Iden3 / Polygon ID** would allow municipal governments to issue cryptographic residency credentials to citizens. 

Under this model, citizens would generate client-side Groth16 or PLONK zero-knowledge proofs directly within their web browser, proving that they possess a valid municipal credential and have not exceeded their voting or reporting frequency limits—without ever revealing their National Identity Number, real name, or persistent public key to the Backend Relayer or smart contract.

\subsection{Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Cross-Modal Verification}
\label{subsec:future-vlms}

To enhance automated moderation accuracy and detect sophisticated civic spam, the AI Moderation Oracle Ensemble should be upgraded to incorporate open-weight **Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** (e.g., fine-tuned LLaVA, Llama-3-Vision, or CLIP-based cross-modal attention architectures).

A VLM oracle worker would simultaneously ingest the citizen's textual description, selected infrastructure category, and uploaded photographic evidence (\texttt{ipfsCid}). The model would compute a semantic cross-modal consistency score to verify whether the image depicts the reported civic defect (e.g., confirming that a report categorized as "Road Hazard" with text describing a pothole actually contains an authentic image of a pothole, rather than stock internet imagery or an unrelated photograph). This cross-modal verification would significantly enhance the platform's automated Sybil and spam resilience.

\subsection{Multi-Organizational Decentralized Oracle Network (DON) Deployment}
\label{subsec:future-don}

To eliminate single-point administrative reliance in off-chain content moderation, future work should transition the AI Oracle layer into a true **Decentralized Oracle Network (DON)**. Instead of executing oracle workers within a single backend infrastructure, individual oracle nodes should be hosted across distinct, autonomous administrative entities—such as local university computer science faculties, independent civic \ac{NGO}s, and municipal IT departments.

By implementing a Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) consensus protocol or threshold signature scheme (such as BLS signatures) among independent oracle operators, a civic report would only be approved for on-chain anchoring after a cryptographic supermajority (e.g., $\ge 2/3 + 1$) of independent nodes independently execute machine learning inference and sign an \texttt{ACCEPT} verdict. This enhancement would guarantee that no single government institution or technical administrator could unilaterally censor or alter civic submissions.

\subsection{Real-World Pilot Field Study and Usability Evaluation}
\label{subsec:future-pilot}

Before city-wide deployment, the system should undergo a structured, multi-phase real-world pilot study within a local municipal council or university campus in Sri Lanka. The field study should evaluate both quantitative system metrics and qualitative user experiences across three stakeholder groups: citizens, municipal engineers (Authority Workers), and municipal administrators (Super Admins).

Key empirical evaluations should include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Usability and Accessibility Metrics:} Measuring System Usability Scale (SUS) scores, task completion rates, and average time-to-report across diverse demographic groups using mobile and desktop browsers.
    \item \textbf{Operational Efficiency Benchmarks:} Evaluating municipal end-to-end resolution latency—comparing the time elapsed from initial report submission to verified closure against legacy municipal reporting channels.
    \item \textbf{Relayer Sustainability and Gas Accounting:} Quantifying relayer treasury gas consumption and automated \texttt{AuthorityFundingService} replenishment rates under real municipal workloads to optimize long-term economic sustainability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:final-remarks}

This thesis demonstrated that the structural deficiencies of centralized local governance reporting platforms can be effectively overcome by synthesizing private permissioned blockchain networks, zero-knowledge privacy protocols, automated relayer orchestration, and artificial intelligence content moderation. By establishing an eight-state deterministic workflow, two-tier multi-signature access control, and Sybil-resistant community voting, the proposed framework transforms civic issue reporting from an opaque, administrative procedure into a transparent, democratic, and mutually accountable civic partnership. 

Although the current prototype represents an intermediate developmental milestone, its architectural rigor and successful verification confirm that privacy-preserving, community-assisted blockchain platforms hold transformative potential for the future of local governance and participatory civil society.
